Six Sigma methodologies · process improvement & new design

The two paths: DMAIC & DMADV

Six Sigma has two major methodologies. DMAIC improves an existing process (Define · Measure · Analyze · Improve · Control). DMADV – also called DFSS – designs something new from scratch (Define · Measure · Analyze · Design · Verify). Pick a case and switch the variant: just like the car company in the video that improves its production (DMAIC) and at the same time designs a new sports car (DMADV). Then go through phase by phase – with meaning, application and the typical tools.

Which variant? DMAIC or DMADV

One question decides: does the process already exist – or are you building it from scratch? Both share D · M · A, then they part ways.

Improve an existing process

DMAIC

A product or process already exists and should get better – fewer defects, faster, more stable. The most common Six Sigma methodology.

There is nothing yet – a new product or service is being created. Also called DFSS (Design for Six Sigma).

3

DMAIC ↔ DMADV side by side

Same start (D · M · A), different end – improve vs. design anew.

Rule of thumb: D · M · A are the same in both methodologies. The difference is at the end: I · C (Improve, Control) repairs and stabilises what exists – D · V (Design, Verify) creates and tests something new.

Context: Lean & Lean Six Sigma

As the video shows at the end – a second school of thought and the combination.

Six Sigma

Reduce variation & defects

Data-driven via DMAIC / DMADV – so that the process becomes predictable.

Lean

Eliminate waste

From Japanese manufacturing (Toyota): remove everything that brings the customer no value. More with less – and better at the same time.

Combination

Lean Six Sigma

Both together: eliminate defects (Six Sigma) and remove waste (Lean) to meet customer needs better.
